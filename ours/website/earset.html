EarSet: what is inside, and how our pipeline sees it

EarSet is a public dataset of in-ear motion recordings from 30 people. We use it as a playground to build and test the pipeline. Nothing on this page is evidence about AirPods: that comes from our own pilot.

1. What was recorded

Each person did 18 recordings of about 2 minutes, one activity each, with a research earbud (IMU at about 100 Hz; we use the left earbud, resampled to 50 Hz). In the three head-gesture recordings the person repeats the gesture again and again. Our detector cuts every recording into motion events; events from the gesture recordings are labelled with that gesture, everything else is "no gesture".

Not in EarSet: left vs right (shake and tilt are not split by direction), up vs down, circles, two-motion commands, and AirPods. "Shake" is our turn.

2. A recording over time

Person P0, first 40 seconds: total rotation speed of the head, with the events our detector cut out shaded. Pick a recording.

3. Single events in the head frame

One event from the middle of each recording, for three people. Rotation speed about the head's three axes: x, nod axis y, tilt axis z, turn axis. A nod lives on x, a shake on z, a tilt on y. Talking and walking are smaller and mixed.

4. Every event as one point: where its rotation goes

The three axis shares add up to 1, so each event is a point in a triangle. A pure nod sits in the nod corner, a pure turn in the turn corner. The 2,035 gesture events form three tight clusters, one per corner, across all 30 people. No-gesture events (a sample of 150 per kind) are spread out, but many also land near a corner: that is why axis shares alone cannot reject talking or walking.

5. Same for everyone, or different per person?

Each dot is one person's median. Left: the share of rotation on the gesture's own axis: 0.84-0.99 for nods and shakes, and for tilts too except a few people (lowest 0.42), whose tilt carries some turning. Right: peak rotation speed: it varies 5-8 fold between people (nod 0.4-2.9 rad/s, shake 1.2-9.0). That is the whole idea behind our feature choice: describe which axis, not how fast.

6. Where each feature's variation comes from (R)

The mixed model splits each feature's variation into gesture, person, style (this person's way of doing this gesture) and trial-to-trial repetition. Only features with a large gesture part pass the keep rule (✓). Source: algorithm/results/variance_components.csv.
